Three front doors, one PlaceIT

Users can reach PlaceIT three different ways. Each one now behaves the way people expect it to, but you only pay to build and maintain it once.

1

The website

getplaceit.com in any browser. Stays fast, searchable on Google, and behaves like a normal website should.

2

Add to home screen

Users tap "Install" in their browser and get an icon on their phone. No app store, no download. Useful in Nigeria where data and storage are tight.

3

The Play Store app

The full Android app, for the credibility and discoverability of being listed in the store.

What's new in the app

Everything below happens only in the app. The website is deliberately left alone. More on why further down.

It copes with a bad connection

Built for the reality of Nigerian mobile data, not an office wifi connection.

  • ✓
    Works offlineLose signal and the app still shows the listings and applications you last loaded, with a small note that it's showing saved information.
  • ✓
    Pull down to refreshDrag down to update. It now refreshes just the information on screen rather than reloading everything. Noticeably faster, and you don't lose your place.
  • ✓
    Honest connection detectionPhones often report "connected" when there's no actual internet. The app now checks properly before telling the user anything.

Why not make the website identical?

It's a fair question, and the answer is that the same feature can help in one place and hurt in the other.

FeatureAppWebsiteReason
Launch screenYesNoMaking a first-time visitor wait two seconds before seeing anything loses them.
Screen transitionsYesNoOn a website this just makes every click feel slower.
Pull to refreshYesNoBrowsers already have their own. Adding ours would replace something that works.
Blocking zoomYesNoBlocking zoom on a public website breaks accessibility rules and is penalised by Google.
Dark modeSigned-in areaNot yetThe public pages use fixed colours chosen by hand, so they need a proper design pass before they can go dark.
Saving data offlineYesNoAppropriate on a personal phone, less so on a shared or public computer.

All of this is controlled from one place, so any of these can be switched on for the website later without rebuilding anything.

Before this goes live

Three things we'd want your input or sign-off on.

Testing on real devices

The back button, vibration and keyboard behaviour can only be properly tested on an actual phone, since they don't exist on a laptop. We'd recommend a round of testing on a few real Android devices before submitting to the Play Store.

Offline mode stores information on the phone

To work without a connection, the app saves what a user last viewed onto their device. It's cleared when they log out and expires after 24 hours.

Worth a quick review together: if there's any information you'd consider too sensitive to sit on a student's phone overnight, tell us and we'll exclude it.

Dark mode covers the signed-in area

Dark mode applies once a user logs in. The public pages stay light for now, because their colours were each chosen by hand and would need a proper design pass rather than an automatic conversion.

Worth someone walking through the signed-in screens in dark mode before launch and flagging anything that looks off.

What we'd recommend next

In the order we'd tackle them, based on impact rather than effort.

  • Push notifications Highest impact

    "Your application was viewed." "A new placement matches your course." This is the main reason someone keeps an app installed rather than bookmarking the website, and the main reason they come back. It's the largest piece of the remaining work, as it needs backend support too.

  • Links that open the app

    Right now, tapping a verification or password-reset link from an email opens the browser instead of the app, which is confusing for someone who installed it. A contained fix.

  • Camera upload for CVs and documents

    Let users photograph a document directly rather than hunting for a file. Removes real friction from onboarding.

  • Dark mode across the public pages

    Extends what's already built to the homepage and marketing pages. This one needs design input first: those pages use individually chosen colours rather than a shared set, so someone has to decide what each becomes.

  • "Get the app" prompt for website visitors

    A gentle banner encouraging people browsing on a phone to install. The groundwork is in place; it just needs the design.

What we need from you

  1. A few Android phones for testing. Ideally a mix of newer and older devices, since that's what your users actually have.
  2. A decision on offline data. Whether anything should be excluded from what's saved on the device.
  3. Someone to review dark mode. Half an hour walking through the signed-in screens with fresh eyes, flagging anything that looks wrong.
  4. A steer on push notifications. Whether that's in scope, since it's the biggest remaining item and needs backend work alongside it.
